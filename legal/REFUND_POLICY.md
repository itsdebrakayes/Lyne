# Refund and Cancellation Policy

**Lyne**

**Last updated:** 21 September 2026

**Effective:** on first release

---

## 1. The principle this policy is built on

**Cancelling is always as easy as subscribing.**

That is the commitment the rest of this document rests on, and it is a commitment with teeth: **if we ever make cancellation harder to find than signing up was, we will not refuse a refund on the grounds that you cancelled late.** A subscription you could not work out how to stop is one we should not have kept charging you for, and we will not take shelter behind our own bad design.

There is no retention queue, no "call us to cancel", no offer you must decline three times, and no cancellation button hidden behind a settings menu you would not think to open. **Whatever it took to start, it takes no more than that to stop** — and it is done in the same place you started.

We put this first rather than last because it is the part that most often goes wrong, and because you should not have to read to the end of a refund policy to find out whether you can leave.

## 3. Refunds on a subscription you bought from us directly

### 3.1 You may cancel at any time

An organisation subscription is arranged by talking to us, so it is cancelled the same way: **write to [SUPPORT EMAIL]**. One email ends it. We do not require notice, a reason, a phone call, or a conversation with anyone trying to talk you out of it.

That symmetry is the point. You did not have to click through a sign-up flow to start, and you do not have to hunt through a settings menu to stop. If we ever add self-service subscribing, we will add self-service cancelling in the same release — not in a later one.

When you cancel, your subscription runs to the end of the period you have already paid for, and then stops. We do not cut off access the moment you cancel — you paid for the month, so you keep the month.

### 3.2 If you have used the service during the period, a full refund is not available

This is the part people most often disagree with, so here is the reasoning rather than just the rule.

A subscription buys you **access to a service for a period of time**. Once your organisation has used that access — staff have signed in, queues have run, customers have been served, reports have been pulled — the service for that period has been delivered. It cannot be given back. A full refund would mean receiving the service and the money both, and that is not something we can offer.

So: **where the service has been used within the period it was bought for, a refund in full cannot be offered.**

### 3.3 A partial refund may be considered, and is judged on the facts

Depending on the specifics of the case, a **partial refund may be considered** — but it is **not guaranteed**, and we will not pretend otherwise by promising a formula we would then have to argue our way out of.

What we weigh:

- **How much of the period was left.** Cancelling on day 2 of a month is not the same as cancelling on day 27.
- **How much the service was actually used.** Light or no use across a period we charged for weighs in your favour.
- **Why you are leaving.** A problem on our side is treated differently from a change of plan on yours.
- **Whether something went wrong that we did not fix.** If it did, section 4 applies instead and this section does not limit it.
- **Whether you were charged for something you had already cancelled.** That is a mistake, and mistakes are refunded in full under section 4.

Write to **[SUPPORT EMAIL]** setting out what happened. We respond within **5 business days** and give you a decision with reasons, not a template. If we decline, we tell you why, and you are free to take it further under section 9.

### 3.4 Where a full refund *is* given

- **You were charged after cancelling.** Refunded in full, always.
- **You were charged twice** for the same period. The duplicate is refunded in full.
- **You have not used the service at all during the period charged** — no sign-ins, no queues run. Refunded in full.
- **Cancellation was obscured, broken, or did not work** when you tried to use it. Refunded in full. See section 1.
- **We ended your subscription** for a reason that was not your breach of the Terms. The unused remainder is refunded.

## 4. When something goes wrong on our side

Nothing in section 3 limits this section.

If the service is **materially defective, unavailable for a sustained period, or not as we described it**, you are entitled to a remedy regardless of how much you have used it. We will fix it, or credit the affected period, or refund it — and where the failure is serious, the choice between those is yours rather than ours.

This reflects your rights under Jamaica's **Consumer Protection Act** and the equivalent law wherever you are. **Nothing in this policy removes a right the law gives you.** Where this document and the law disagree, the law wins, and you should read the clause as saying whatever the law requires.

Planned maintenance that we told you about in advance is not an outage for this purpose. An unplanned failure lasting more than a few hours is.

## 5. Free trials

If a paid plan is offered with a trial:

- We tell you the **exact date** the trial ends and the **exact amount** you will be charged, before it starts.
- We remind you by email **at least 3 days before** the first charge.
- Cancelling during the trial costs nothing and is one click.
- If we fail to send that reminder and you are charged, **the first charge is refunded in full on request.** That is our failure, not yours.

## 6. Purchases made through the App Store or Google Play

If you bought a paid feature inside the mobile app, **Apple or Google took your money, not us.** We cannot see your card, we cannot process the refund, and we cannot overrule their decision. This is how both stores require it to work; it is not us passing you around.

- **Apple / App Store:** request a refund at `reportaproblem.apple.com`, or through Settings on your device. Apple decides, under its own terms.
- **Google Play:** request a refund through Google Play's order history or its refund form. Google offers self-service refunds within a short window after purchase and reviews later requests case by case.
- **Cancelling the subscription itself** is done in your device's subscription settings — Apple and Google both require this, and neither lets us cancel on your behalf.

**What we will do:** if a store refuses a refund and you believe the reason was something we did — the app did not work, a feature was not as described — write to **[SUPPORT EMAIL]**. We will look at it and, where we agree, we will support your case to the store or make it right another way. We will not simply tell you it is Apple's problem.

## 7. How to ask, and what happens

1. Write to **[SUPPORT EMAIL]** with the account or organisation name and what you are asking for.
2. We acknowledge within **2 business days**.
3. We decide within **5 business days** of having what we need, and tell you the reason either way.
4. An approved refund is issued to the **original payment method**, and normally reaches you within **5 to 10 business days** after we send it — the last stretch is your bank's timing, not ours.

You do not need to accept credit instead of money. If a refund is due, you can have it as a refund.

## 8. What this policy does not cover

**The service you actually attended the branch for.** Lyne manages the queue; it does not provide the tax assessment, the passport, the loan interview, or the registration you were queueing for. If you paid that organisation for something and want your money back, the organisation refunds it — we have no ability to, and no visibility of it.

**Losses from a missed appointment.** Waiting times in Lyne are estimates, not bookings, and the Terms say so. If a queue moved faster than predicted and you missed your turn, that is not a refundable event. Where an outage on our side caused it, section 4 applies.

## 9. If you disagree with our decision

Come back to us first — say why, and a person will look at it again rather than re-sending the first answer.

If that does not resolve it, you may take the matter to the **Consumer Affairs Commission (CAC)** in Jamaica, or to the equivalent consumer body where you are. You can go to them directly; you do not need our permission and you do not have to exhaust our process first.

Please do not start a **card chargeback** before talking to us. A chargeback freezes the dispute into a process neither of us controls, and it is usually slower than simply asking. Your right to raise one is untouched — we would just rather fix it.

## 10. Changes

We may update this policy. If a change **reduces** your rights, it applies only to subscription periods beginning after we have given you at least **30 days' notice** by email — it is never applied backwards to money you have already paid. Improvements take effect immediately.
