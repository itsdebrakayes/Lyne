## 2. What this app does

Q Me Now lets you see how busy a public or private service branch is, join a queue remotely, hold your place, and be told when to set off and when you are called. Organisations use a separate administrator application to run their counters.

## 3. Whose data we hold, and in what capacity

This is the most important section for understanding your rights, because we hold data about three different groups and our legal role is not the same for each.

| Group | Who they are | Our role |
|---|---|---|
| **App users** | Members of the public who install Q Me Now and join queues | **Controller** — we decide why and how their data is used |
| **Visitors served at a counter** | People a member of staff adds to a queue at a kiosk or desk, who may never use our app | **Processor**, acting on the organisation's instructions |
| **Staff users** | Employees of a customer organisation with an administrator or counter account | **Processor** for their queue activity; **controller** for their account and security records |

**Where we are a processor**, the organisation — the tax office, court, campus or credit union — is the controller. It decides what is collected and why; we act on its documented instructions. If you are in that group and want your data corrected or erased, ask the organisation. If you ask us, we will pass the request to them and tell you we have done so.

**Where we are a controller**, this policy governs, and you exercise your rights directly with us under section 10.

### 4.2 Generated by using the service

- Tickets you take: number, verification code, position, times joined/called/served
- Visit history: which branch, which service, when
- Registrations for a scheduled session, and whether you checked in
- Organisations you save
- Ratings and feedback you submit
- Notification preferences and device push tokens

### 4.3 Collected automatically

- **Approximate location**, only while the app is open and only if you allow it, to estimate travel time and show nearby branches. You can refuse, and the app still works.
- Device and app information: model, OS version, app version, language
- Crash and error diagnostics
- Log data: IP address, timestamps, endpoints called

### 4.4 Sensitive personal data

TRN, National ID and passport numbers are **sensitive personal data** under the DPA. We:

- collect them **only if you choose** to add them — every queue feature works without,
- use them **only** to identify you to the organisation serving you,
- never sell, rent or share them for marketing, ever,
- let you delete them individually, without deleting your account.

### 4.5 What we deliberately do not collect

So the boundary is explicit:

- We do not collect the **reason** for your visit beyond the service you selected. We do not know what your court matter is about, what your medical appointment is for, or the state of your account.
- We do not record audio or video.
- We do not track you across other apps or websites, and we do not run advertising networks.
- Where a portal checks a reference number against a list an organisation gave us, we check the number. We **do not** need or keep an identity document to do it.

### 4.6 Children

Q Me Now is not intended for under-18s and we do not knowingly collect their data. If you believe a child has given us data, contact us and we will delete it.

## 6. Automated processing

We use statistical models to forecast waits and suggest quieter times. These predictions inform *you*; they do not make decisions about you, do not affect your place in any queue, and produce no legal or similarly significant effect. There is no automated decision-making within the meaning of the DPA.

Where a session portal checks a reference number against a list supplied by an organisation, the check is a lookup, not a judgement about you. If it finds nothing, you are told so and given the organisation's contact details — a person decides, not the system.

## 10. Your rights

Under the DPA you have the right to:

- **Be informed** — this policy
- **Access** a copy of your data
- **Rectify** anything inaccurate — most of it is editable in the app
- **Erase** your data — Profile → Delete my account
- **Restrict** or **object to** processing, including a right to object to processing based on legitimate interests
- **Portability** — a machine-readable copy
- **Withdraw consent** at any time, without affecting what came before
- **Complain** to the **Office of the Information Commissioner** — you can do that without contacting us first

### 10.1 How to make a request, and what happens

Write to **[PRIVACY EMAIL]**. You do not need a particular form of words.

1. We acknowledge within **5 working days**.
2. We may ask you to confirm your identity — only enough to be sure, and we do not keep what you send for that purpose.
3. We respond within **30 days**. If a request is complex we may extend once, and will tell you why before the 30 days are up.
4. It is **free**. We will only charge for a manifestly unfounded or repetitive request, and we will say so first rather than invoice you afterwards.
5. If we refuse, we will tell you which exemption we rely on and how to challenge it, including your right to go to the Information Commissioner.

### 10.2 Deleting your account

Profile → **Delete my account**. This permanently erases your profile, saved organisations, visit history, notifications, saved cards, and **every identity document and scan we hold**, and gives up any queue you are in.

Organisations keep an anonymised record that a visit occurred, for their own service statistics. It contains nothing that identifies you and is no longer personal data.

## 11. How we protect it

- Encryption in transit (HTTPS/TLS) and at rest
- Passwords never stored by us; handled by our authentication provider
- Card numbers never touch our servers
- Access controlled by role and by organisation — staff can only reach their own organisation's data, enforced server-side on every request, not merely hidden in the interface
- Optional Face ID / passcode lock on your saved documents
- Identifiers and codes stripped from diagnostic reports before they leave your device
- Audit logging of administrative access
- Rate limiting on public lookup endpoints, so a reference number cannot be guessed at scale

### 11.1 If something goes wrong

No system is perfectly secure. If a breach occurs:

- we contain and assess it immediately;
- where it is likely to affect your rights, we notify the **Office of the Information Commissioner without undue delay**, and in any event within the period the DPA requires;
- where the risk to you is high, we tell **you** directly and plainly — what happened, what it means for you, and what to do;
- where we are a processor, we notify the organisation without undue delay so it can meet its own obligations;
- we record every breach, including ones we are not required to report.
