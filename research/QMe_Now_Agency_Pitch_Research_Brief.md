# QMe Now — Agency Pitch Research Brief

**Research started:** 29 July 2026  
**Initial targets:** Tax Administration Jamaica (TAJ), National Housing Trust (NHT), Passport, Immigration and Citizenship Agency (PICA)

## 1. The strongest early positioning

QMe Now should not be sold as “another online portal.” TAJ, NHT and PICA already offer online services, and each is continuing to digitise transactions.

The stronger position is:

> **QMe Now is the live operating layer for every customer who still needs service — online or in person. It shows the public when and where to go, shows staff what to do next, and shows management where service is breaking down before complaints pile up.**

Existing portals help a customer complete a transaction or track a case. QMe Now manages the demand, physical queues, counters, staffing and service performance surrounding those transactions.

That distinction will be essential in the deck, FAQ and demo:

- **TAJ Online collects payments; QMe Now manages the people and services that still require a Tax Office.**
- **PICA REL-M manages customer cases and enquiries; QMe Now manages today’s appointments, walk-ins, service lines and counters.**
- **NHT Online manages applications, account information and tracking; QMe Now manages in-branch advice, exceptions, document checks and branch capacity.**

## 2. What QMe Now is, in plain language

QMe Now lets a person:

- see which branch is open and how long each service is taking;
- join a line before travelling;
- watch their live position;
- receive an alert as their turn approaches;
- know what documents to bring;
- use a branch clerk to join the same line if they do not use the app.

QMe Now lets an organisation:

- run one fair line for app users and walk-ins;
- see each service, counter and branch in real time;
- move trained staff to the service under pressure;
- compare performance with service targets;
- see peak periods and likely demand;
- identify no-shows, stalled counters and unusual delays;
- give executives one view across the whole network.

These capabilities are supported by the current product in the workspace: consumer queueing, a walk-in kiosk, line-staff/supervisor/manager/executive views, live counter-aware wait estimates, targets, heatmaps, staffing recommendations, no-show risk, anomaly alerts and multi-branch reports.

## 3. The “it keeps learning” story

Recommended plain-language explanation:

> **QMe Now learns how each branch and service really behaves. It notices which days and hours become busy, how long different services usually take, when people are likely to miss their turn and where more counters are needed. As conditions change, it refreshes its recommendations using the newest operating data.**

What the current product actually does:

- uses the live line and number of open counters to give an immediate wait estimate;
- learns from visit history by branch, service, day and time;
- forecasts demand;
- recommends the number of counters needed to hold waits below a target;
- identifies unusual operating conditions;
- refreshes the predictive pipeline on start-up and every two hours;
- records when each recommendation was generated so managers can see its freshness.

Honesty guardrail:

- Do not promise that QMe Now “knows everything on day one.”
- A pilot begins with live queue calculations and operational rules.
- Recommendations improve as the agency builds real service history.
- Accuracy and target improvements must be validated with pilot data before numerical outcome claims are used in a sales deck.

## 4. The five cross-agency pain points

These are the best-supported starting points for the “you have five problems / QMe Now solves five problems” pitch.

### 1. Online services have not removed peak walk-in traffic

TAJ collects approximately 80% of its revenue online, yet the Government expanded Saturday Tax Office operations to ease weekday congestion and reduce wait time. This proves that digital payment adoption and physical service pressure coexist.

**QMe Now response:** show live demand by service and branch, let customers wait away from the office, and spread visits toward quieter locations and times.

### 2. Managers cannot act quickly without a live operating picture

Agency plans and job descriptions repeatedly call for service metrics, branch targets, peak planning, daily staffing, business intelligence and operational efficiency. The missing sales message is not “more data”; it is **one clear answer to what needs attention now**.

**QMe Now response:** show the worst service first and recommend a specific action, such as moving a trained officer to a busy line or opening another counter.

### 3. Staffing and demand do not always meet in the same place

Traffic changes by branch, service, deadline, day and hour. Extending opening hours is useful but expensive if managers cannot see the exact service causing the delay.

**QMe Now response:** forecast busy periods, distinguish service-level demand, and recommend how many counters should be open to meet a wait-time target.

### 4. Incomplete information creates repeat visits, delays and frustration

PICA and NHT both explicitly warn that non-compliant applications, missing documents and inaccurate information cause processing delays. Customers often discover a problem only after travelling and waiting.

**QMe Now response:** show service-specific document guidance before the visit and use the queue journey to send clear reminders. This should be positioned as delay prevention, not as replacing agency validation.

### 5. Service standards exist, but performance is difficult to see continuously

Jamaica’s Service Excellence Policy proposes measures including first response time, average wait, first-contact resolution, total resolution time and the number of contacts required. PICA and NHT publish their own service commitments.

**QMe Now response:** turn standards into live targets at the branch and executive levels, so the organisation can intervene during the day rather than only reviewing complaints after the fact.

## 5. Agency research and pitch angles

## A. Tax Administration Jamaica (TAJ)

### Evidence-backed pressure points

- TAJ collects approximately **80% of revenue through online platforms**, but physical offices are still needed for assisted and document-based services.
- Saturday openings were expanded specifically to **alleviate high weekday walk-in traffic**, improve customer service and reduce wait time.
- More than **29,000 people** used Saturday openings between April and August 2025, generating more than **J$567 million**.
- The Government committed **J$11 billion** to tax-office modernisation, including new and expanded facilities to support increased demand.
- Driver’s licence services can begin online, but documents still have to be submitted at a Tax Office.
- Public reporting in 2025 described people selling early queue numbers to customers desperate to skip long Tax Office lines. This is not an official performance measure, but it is a strong signal of perceived unfairness and customer frustration.

### Language TAJ already uses

- taxpayer convenience;
- easier access;
- alleviate high walk-in traffic;
- ease congestion;
- reduce wait time;
- improve revenue collection;
- serve the public better;
- online and in-person options.

### Five TAJ-specific problems to test

1. Peak-day congestion remains even with high online payment adoption.
2. A customer cannot see the live wait by office and service before travelling.
3. Management needs service-level visibility, not only total office traffic.
4. Staff and counters may not be aligned with the service under pressure.
5. Number-ticket systems can create uncertainty and perceived unfairness.

### QMe Now answer

- one verified line for app users and walk-ins;
- live wait times for TRN, driver’s licence, property tax and other in-person services;
- due-date and month-end demand forecasting;
- service-level staffing recommendations;
- live comparison of Tax Offices;
- alerts when a service exceeds its target;
- digital ticket verification that makes queue manipulation more difficult.

### Best TAJ demo story

1. Start with the customer seeing live waits at multiple Tax Offices.
2. Show the customer joining the TRN line remotely and receiving a live position.
3. Add a walk-in through the kiosk to prove the system does not exclude people without the app.
4. Switch to the manager dashboard: TRN has 14 people waiting, with only two of four windows open.
5. Show the recommendation to move a TRN-trained staff member to the open counter.
6. End on the executive view: which offices are above target, peak periods and the improvement after action.

### Positioning guardrail

Do not imply that QMe Now replaces TAJ Online or its payment systems. The pitch is that QMe Now protects the value of TAJ’s digital investment by managing the remaining in-person demand more intelligently.

## B. Passport, Immigration and Citizenship Agency (PICA)

### Evidence-backed pressure points

- PICA’s May 2024 Citizen’s Charter says it will maintain a queuing system at each office and that regional-office customers require appointments.
- The Charter covers services with very different processing patterns: passport applications, collections, immigration and citizenship.
- PICA’s Customer Service and Operations Manager is explicitly responsible for daily staffing, branch targets, peak plans, workflow adjustment, service metrics and customer-service dashboards.
- PICA implemented REL-M, a customer relationship management system, and reported more than **44,000 tickets processed** through it by February 2025.
- PICA says REL-M data will support decision-making, while its current operations role calls for data-driven insight and real-time adjustment.
- PICA expects customers to submit compliant documents and photographs to prevent delays.

### Language PICA already uses

- customer-oriented;
- strategy-focused;
- technology-driven;
- operational agility;
- peak planning;
- service delivery standards;
- customer satisfaction;
- efficient delivery across all locations;
- workflow and process improvement;
- data-driven insights.

### Five PICA-specific problems to test

1. Appointments and walk-ins must be managed together without losing fairness.
2. Fast collection services and slower application services create different bottlenecks.
3. Peak periods require staff scheduling and counter changes during the day.
4. Missing or non-compliant documents create avoidable delays.
5. REL-M gives case information, but branch managers still need a live view of physical service flow.

### QMe Now answer

- manage appointments and walk-ins in one visible service flow;
- separate lines by actual service, such as new passport, renewal and collection;
- show which service is holding up the branch;
- recommend counter and staff changes during peaks;
- show document requirements before travel;
- compare service targets across locations;
- forecast likely demand and no-shows using the branch’s own history.

### Best PICA demo story

1. Show a customer choosing between new passport, renewal and collection.
2. Show the required-document guidance before joining.
3. Show an appointment/walk-in service view at the branch.
4. Reveal that Passport Collection has crossed its target while another counter has capacity.
5. Reassign a trained staff member and show the expected effect on the line.
6. End with the executive view across Constant Spring and regional offices.

### Positioning guardrail

Present QMe Now as a complement to REL-M:

> **REL-M helps PICA manage the customer’s case. QMe Now helps PICA manage the customer’s time and the branch’s capacity.**

## C. National Housing Trust (NHT)

### Evidence-backed pressure points

- NHT’s 2025/26 operational plan calls for improved operating efficiency and service delivery through technology.
- It explicitly targets reduced wait time for loan applications and processing, a contactless application process, automated contributor validation, online application tracking and remote queries.
- NHT planned to process **7,046 mortgage loans** in 2025/26 and projected a staff complement of **1,411**.
- NHT’s Citizen’s Charter says it will try to see walk-ins within 30 minutes or offer the option to continue waiting or make an appointment.
- Correctly submitted contribution refunds have a 15-working-day service standard.
- NHT repeatedly warns that inaccurate information and missing documents delay refund processing.
- The January refund period creates a predictable surge. Public reporting in January 2025 noted over 30,000 applications in the first three days of the prior year and disruption to the mobile-app channel.

### Language NHT already uses

- service culture;
- operational efficiency;
- enhance service delivery;
- reduce wait time;
- contactless application;
- online application tracking;
- remote query facility;
- safety and satisfaction of contributors;
- accurate and complete information.

### Five NHT-specific problems to test

1. Online processing does not remove the need for advice, exceptions and document help.
2. Refund periods and housing-programme launches create sharp demand spikes.
3. Loan applications, benefits enquiries and simple payments take very different amounts of staff time.
4. Incomplete applications cause repeat contact and delayed service.
5. The organisation needs to know whether branches are meeting the 30-minute walk-in commitment.

### QMe Now answer

- direct contributors to the right branch, service and time;
- separate quick enquiries from longer loan-advice sessions;
- forecast January refund and programme-driven demand;
- align trained advisors with the services that need them;
- remind customers what to bring;
- measure the walk-in target live;
- provide an executive view across the branch network.

### Best NHT demo story

1. Show a contributor choosing Benefits Enquiry, Contribution Refund or Loan Application.
2. Show branch-specific wait times and the best time to visit.
3. Show the document checklist before the customer travels.
4. Switch to the manager view, where a long loan-advice line is hidden by a low total headcount.
5. Show the staffing recommendation and the expected target effect.
6. End with January demand patterns and the organisation-wide service-standard view.

### Positioning guardrail

Do not pitch QMe Now as an end-to-end loan processing system. NHT is already investing in that. Pitch QMe Now as the operating layer for the human service around the application: advice, exceptions, document readiness, appointments, walk-ins and branch capacity.

## 6. “Why the organisation cannot do without it”

The answer should avoid inflated language. A credible version is:

> **Because digital services alone do not tell you what is happening in the branch right now. Without QMe Now, customers still travel without knowing the wait, managers still discover bottlenecks after the line has formed, and executives still review service problems after the complaints arrive. QMe Now turns every visit into a live signal and every signal into a clear action.**

The organisational case rests on five outcomes:

- **visibility:** one operating picture across services and branches;
- **control:** a manager can act before a line becomes a crisis;
- **fairness:** app users and walk-ins follow one visible process;
- **accountability:** service standards become measurable daily targets;
- **adaptability:** recommendations change as real demand changes.

## 7. FAQ questions the research says buyers will ask

These should be developed as a separate FAQ document.

1. Is QMe Now replacing our current portal, CRM or core system?
2. How does it work for customers without smartphones or data?
3. How are walk-ins and remote customers kept in a fair order?
4. What happens if a customer misses their turn?
5. How accurate are the wait times?
6. What exactly does the system learn?
7. How long does it need before recommendations improve?
8. Can managers override a recommendation?
9. Can the system work with our existing service targets and branch structure?
10. Can it integrate with our current systems?
11. What data does QMe Now collect?
12. Where is the data stored, and who can see it?
13. How does QMe Now comply with Jamaica’s Data Protection Act?
14. What is the fallback if internet or a branch system is unavailable?
15. How will a pilot be measured?
16. What training will staff need?
17. How does QMe Now prevent line-jumping or ticket sharing?
18. What does implementation cost, and what internal resources are required?

## 8. Security, privacy and procurement objections

Government buyers will expect these issues before they accept the customer-experience story.

Current QMe Now strengths:

- separate agency deployment and database;
- role-based access;
- branch, queue and ticket access controls;
- authentication, rate limiting and session controls;
- audit logging;
- limited public exposure of ticket information.

Current work that must be completed or formally documented before procurement:

- privacy notice;
- retention and deletion schedule;
- confirmed encryption-at-rest position;
- agency/data-controller and QMe Now/data-processor responsibilities;
- breach-response procedure;
- data processing agreement;
- pilot security assessment;
- business-continuity and manual-fallback procedure.

Jamaica’s Office of the Information Commissioner states that data controllers must register, public authorities are a priority group, data must be minimised, kept only as long as necessary, protected by technical and organisational measures, and relevant breaches must be reported within 72 hours. These requirements should be treated as part of the product and procurement story, not buried in technical notes.

## 9. Research still needed before the final deck

The public evidence supports the problem hypotheses, but the final agency deck should not invent internal facts. For each target organisation, request or discover:

- average and 90th-percentile wait by branch and service;
- hourly/daily arrival volumes;
- number of counters and staff skills by service;
- appointment versus walk-in volumes;
- no-show and abandonment rates;
- repeat visits caused by missing documents;
- service-target compliance;
- complaint themes;
- current queue-system vendor and integration options;
- busiest statutory dates or seasonal events;
- branch disruptions and re-routing process;
- data hosting, retention and security requirements;
- the decision-maker, operational sponsor, IT/security reviewer and procurement path.

The pilot proposal should then attach outcomes to the agency’s own baseline, for example:

- percentage of customers served within the agency target;
- average and 90th-percentile wait;
- customer abandonment;
- staff utilisation by service;
- number of same-day counter reassignments;
- forecast accuracy;
- customer satisfaction and repeat-contact rate.

## 11. Recommended deliverable sequence

Keep these as four separate sales assets:

1. **One-page elevator pitch** — the simple case for QMe Now.
2. **Agency-specific deck** — pain points in the organisation’s language, QMe Now’s response, how it works, continuous learning, indispensable value and a pilot ask.
3. **FAQ document** — operational, integration, security, privacy, implementation and commercial objections.
4. **Demo runbook** — exact screens, sample scenario, talk track and proof point for each agency.
