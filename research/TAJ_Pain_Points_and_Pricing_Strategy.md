# Lyne — TAJ Pain Points and Pricing Strategy

**Prepared:** 29 July 2026  
**Purpose:** Research-backed message and commercial model for a first engagement with Tax Administration Jamaica

## Executive recommendation

Sell TAJ a **paid, fixed-scope pilot**, not a free trial and not an immediate national contract.

The recommended commercial structure is:

1. **Founding-client pilot:** one fixed price for a narrow, measurable deployment.
2. **Production rollout:** a one-time implementation and configuration fee.
3. **Annual platform agreement:** licence, hosting, updates, monitoring and standard support.
4. **Optional enhanced support retainer:** only if TAJ wants faster response times or regular on-site support.
5. **Additional training and custom work:** priced separately after the included launch package is used.

This is easier for a government buyer to understand than a setup fee, monthly subscription, maintenance fee and retainer all charged separately.

## The five TAJ pain points Lyne actually solves

These are not generic assumptions. They use language TAJ and the Government of Jamaica have already used to describe Tax Office operations.

## 1. “Usually high walk-in traffic” and weekday congestion

### How TAJ describes it

TAJ said its Saturday opening initiative was intended to alleviate the **“usually high walk-in traffic”** during busy weekdays. The Minister of Finance described the objective as helping to **“ease the congestion in the week”** and reduce wait time.

This remains a live problem even though TAJ reports that approximately 80% of revenue is collected online. More than 29,000 people used the Saturday openings between April and August 2025.

### What is really hurting

- taxpayers travel without knowing how busy a particular office or service is;
- crowds form before staff can react;
- TAJ absorbs the cost of extended and Saturday opening;
- a busy office can look like one problem even when one specific service is causing most of the delay.

### What Lyne changes

- taxpayers see the live wait by Tax Office and service before travelling;
- customers can join the line remotely and wait away from the building;
- managers see which service is creating the congestion;
- the system directs customers toward quieter branches and times;
- walk-ins join the same managed line through the branch kiosk.

### Deck headline

> **TAJ has expanded access to ease weekday congestion. Lyne helps prevent that congestion from forming.**

### Demo proof

Show the taxpayer comparing branches, seeing the TRN wait and joining remotely. Then show a walk-in added to the same queue by a clerk.

## 2. TAJ needs “tracking of service demand” and “real time information”

### How TAJ describes it

The Government stated that TAJ’s automated queue initiative was intended to track service demand and provide real-time information for improved queue management.

That language describes Lyne’s central value almost exactly.

### What is really hurting

- a paper or number-ticket line tells staff who is next but does not explain why the line is growing;
- office totals hide the service, counter or hour causing the problem;
- executives receive reports after the service failure rather than an actionable warning during it.

### What Lyne changes

- live service demand by branch, queue and counter;
- longest-waiting and over-target services shown first;
- alerts when a counter stalls or a service crosses its target;
- branch comparisons and executive reporting;
- clear recommended actions instead of another data-heavy report.

### Deck headline

> **TAJ asked for real-time service-demand information. Lyne turns that information into the next action.**

### Demo proof

Show the manager screen identifying TRN as the pressure point: 14 waiting, only two of four windows open, with a named recommendation to open another trained counter.

## 3. The most heavily trafficked offices need staffing that follows demand

### How TAJ describes it

TAJ has used flexi-time, earlier opening and Saturday operations for heavily trafficked offices. This shows that the operating problem is not only the line; it is matching staff availability and operating hours to actual demand.

### What is really hurting

- demand changes by deadline, service, branch, weekday and hour;
- extending hours across an entire office is costly when the pressure may sit in one service;
- trained staff can be idle in one area while customers wait in another;
- managers often react after the line has already compounded.

### What Lyne changes

- forecasts peak days and hours using TAJ’s own service history;
- recommends how many counters are needed to hold a chosen wait-time target;
- identifies trained staff who can be reassigned;
- shows safe periods for breaks, training and administrative work;
- updates recommendations as operating conditions change.

### Deck headline

> **More opening hours help. Putting the right trained officer at the right window helps more.**

### Demo proof

Show a trained but unassigned officer and the system recommendation to move that person to TRN until the peak subsides.

## 4. The transition to online service will take time

### How TAJ describes it

TAJ has reported strong online collection, while the Minister of Finance acknowledged that it takes time to transition some people to online service. TAJ also recognises that some customers prefer or require in-person help.

### What is really hurting

- not every taxpayer is comfortable with online-only service;
- some transactions still require documents, identity checks or collection;
- an online-first strategy can leave walk-ins feeling excluded;
- running separate digital and walk-in processes can create confusion and perceived unfairness.

### What Lyne changes

- one operational line for remote customers and walk-ins;
- clerk-assisted kiosk entry for people who do not use the app;
- the customer receives the same position logic regardless of entry channel;
- branch staff can distinguish walk-in and online demand without creating separate, competing systems.

### Deck headline

> **Lyne advances digital service without leaving walk-in taxpayers behind.**

### Demo proof

Place one app customer and one clerk-assisted walk-in into the same service flow and show how both positions are managed.

## 5. Online steps reduce—but do not eliminate—time spent in the Tax Office

### How TAJ describes it

TAJ says customers can start some processes online to **“minimise the time spent in-branch.”** Driver’s licence renewal is one example: payment and form preparation can begin online, but documents still have to be submitted at an office.

### What is really hurting

- online and physical steps form one customer journey, but the customer experiences them as disconnected;
- customers may arrive without the right document or at the wrong office;
- quick collection and payment services can be trapped behind longer application services;
- TAJ cannot fully realise the value of online preparation if the remaining branch visit is still unpredictable.

### What Lyne changes

- service-specific document reminders before travel;
- a separate queue and wait estimate for the exact transaction;
- quick services are not hidden inside a general line;
- the customer receives a live position and arrival alert;
- management can measure whether the online preparation actually reduced branch time.

### Deck headline

> **TAJ has shortened the online part of the journey. Lyne makes the remaining branch visit predictable.**

### Demo proof

Show a driver’s licence customer reviewing the required documents, choosing the correct service and joining a service-specific line rather than a general Tax Office queue.

## The single sentence pitch to TAJ

> **Lyne gives TAJ the real-time service-demand and queue-management capability it has already identified as necessary—while helping taxpayers spend less time at Tax Offices and helping managers use existing staff and counters more effectively.**

## Why TAJ should care immediately

The financial and operational connection is:

> **A Tax Office line is not only a customer-service problem. It affects taxpayer convenience, staff cost, use of facilities, perceptions of fairness and the ease with which Jamaicans remain compliant.**

The product should therefore be sold against:

- percentage served within the agreed wait target;
- average and 90th-percentile wait by service;
- weekday and month-end congestion;
- staff utilisation by service;
- number of customers who leave before service;
- number of customers redirected to quieter branches or periods;
- difference between app and walk-in service outcomes;
- customer satisfaction;
- operating cost of overtime and extended opening.

## Recommended pilot

### Founding-client pilot price

**Recommended offer: J$2.75 million–J$2.95 million total for a tightly scoped 90-day pilot.**

Use the upper end as the quoted price and hold the lower end as negotiating room.

The Ministry of Finance has announced a J$3 million threshold below which single-source procurement may be permissible for goods and services. TAJ’s procurement team must confirm the applicable method and whether the threshold is tax-inclusive. The pilot must be a genuine, self-contained proof-of-value engagement; the project must not be artificially divided to avoid competition.

### Pilot scope

- one high-volume Tax Office;
- up to three priority services;
- up to 25 named staff users;
- one manager view and one executive sponsor view;
- remote queue joining and branch-assisted walk-in entry;
- live queue and wait-time monitoring;
- initial service targets and alerts;
- baseline measurement before launch;
- 90 days of use;
- weekly operating review;
- end-of-pilot results and rollout recommendation.

### Included launch training

- one executive/leadership orientation;
- two manager/supervisor workshops;
- up to four frontline training sessions;
- one train-the-trainer session for TAJ’s internal champions;
- quick-reference guides and recorded walkthroughs;
- launch-week office hours and two weeks of adoption support.

The proposal should say **“initial adoption and training package included”**, not “free training.” Its cost is part of the pilot fee.

### Not included

- integration with RAiS or another TAJ core system;
- national rollout;
- custom hardware, tablets, displays or printers;
- SMS charges or other third-party usage fees;
- 24/7 support;
- major feature development;
- extensive data migration;
- travel and accommodation outside the agreed pilot location.

These exclusions prevent the founding pilot from turning into an unlimited implementation.

### Normal non-discounted pilot

If TAJ requests multiple locations, core-system integration, production-level security certification, hardware or a longer pilot, the appropriate range is **J$4.5 million–J$7.5 million**, with the procurement method determined by TAJ.

## Should TAJ pay for the pilot?

**Yes.**

Provide these at no charge:

- an introductory meeting;
- a tailored presentation;
- a controlled product demonstration;
- one short discovery session;
- a preliminary pilot outline.

Charge for anything that involves:

- configuring TAJ services or branches;
- using TAJ data;
- onboarding staff;
- training;
- live taxpayer use;
- hosting;
- monitoring;
- support;
- reporting results.

A free operational pilot creates four risks:

- it is easier for the agency to deprioritise;
- the scope can expand without control;
- Lyne carries privacy, security and support risk without revenue;
- “free” makes it harder to establish the commercial value later.

If a concession is necessary, offer a **founding-client discount**, not a free pilot. In exchange, request subject to TAJ approval:

- timely access to operational stakeholders;
- an agreed feedback schedule;
- permission to use anonymised performance results;
- a reference or case study after successful completion;
- an opportunity to present a production-rollout proposal, subject to public-procurement rules.

Do not give away ownership of the Lyne platform, exclusivity or unlimited custom development.

## Production pricing after a successful pilot

The best model for a hosted government platform is:

> **One-time implementation fee + annual platform, hosting and standard-support agreement.**

Avoid charging a platform subscription and a separate routine-maintenance retainer for the same work. Buyers will see that as double charging.

### 1. One-time implementation and rollout

| Rollout scope | Recommended range |
|---|---:|
| First 3–5 offices | J$8 million–J$15 million |
| 6–15 offices | J$15 million–J$25 million |
| National rollout, up to approximately 30 offices | J$25 million–J$40 million |

The exact fee depends on integrations, security requirements, hardware, data migration, branch variation and travel.

Implementation includes:

- requirements validation and rollout design;
- agency and branch configuration;
- security and privacy planning;
- user setup and access roles;
- agreed integrations;
- testing and acceptance;
- launch training;
- go-live support;
- deployment documentation.

### 2. Annual platform agreement

| Active network | Recommended annual fee |
|---|---:|
| Up to 5 offices | J$9 million |
| 6–15 offices | J$15 million |
| Up to 30 offices | J$24 million |

The annual fee should include:

- the Lyne platform licence;
- secure hosting;
- backups and uptime monitoring;
- routine software updates;
- security patches;
- model refresh and analytics processing;
- standard weekday support;
- quarterly service reviews;
- one annual refresher programme;
- agreed storage and usage allowance.

Third-party costs above the allowance—such as SMS, unusually high storage, external identity services or specialist security tools—should be passed through transparently.

### 3. Optional enhanced support retainer

Standard support belongs inside the annual fee. Charge an additional retainer only where TAJ requests enhanced service.

| Enhanced service | Suggested fee |
|---|---:|
| Priority support with four-hour critical response | J$300,000 per month |
| Extended-hours support plus scheduled on-site presence | J$500,000–J$750,000 per month |

Define:

- support hours;
- response and restoration targets;
- number of included on-site days;
- escalation contacts;
- exclusions;
- after-hours rates.

## Training pricing

Initial training should be included in implementation, but the proposal must set participant and session limits.

After launch:

| Additional training | Suggested fee |
|---|---:|
| Half-day remote session, up to 20 participants | J$175,000 |
| Half-day on-site session, up to 20 participants | J$275,000 plus travel |
| Full-day role-based workshop, up to 20 participants | J$450,000 plus travel |
| New-office launch and train-the-trainer package | J$600,000–J$900,000 |

Include one refresher programme in the annual agreement. Charge separately for:

- new staff cohorts beyond the allowance;
- repeated on-site sessions;
- training outside normal business hours;
- new-office launches;
- customised manuals or certification;
- travel outside the agreed area.

## Payment structure

### Pilot

- 40% on contract signature and purchase order;
- 30% after configuration and acceptance testing;
- 20% at live pilot launch;
- 10% on delivery of the final results report.

### Production rollout

- 30% mobilisation;
- 30% after approved configuration and integrations;
- 25% as offices go live;
- 15% on final acceptance.

### Annual platform agreement

Prefer annual payment in advance. If TAJ requires instalments, accept quarterly payment in advance.

Do not start live work without:

- a signed agreement;
- an approved statement of work;
- a purchase order or other authorised commitment;
- named TAJ project and procurement contacts;
- agreed acceptance criteria;
- agreed payment dates.

## How being a new professional affects the sale

It will affect the size and risk of the first engagement more than the value of the product.

TAJ may be concerned about:

- limited organisational track record;
- capacity to support a national agency;
- cybersecurity and privacy maturity;
- continuity if the founder is unavailable;
- implementation and change-management experience;
- procurement eligibility.

The answer is not to become the cheapest bidder. Reduce TAJ’s risk:

- sell one measurable pilot;
- give it a clear beginning, end and acceptance criteria;
- use a founding-client discount;
- document security, privacy, backups and incident response;
- identify a second technical/support resource;
- use train-the-trainer so the agency is not dependent on one person;
- consider a credible Jamaican implementation or cybersecurity partner;
- retain ownership of the core platform;
- use the pilot to earn the reference required for a larger contract.

Do not lead with “I am new.” Lead with:

> **This will be a founder-led pilot with direct access to the product team, a tightly controlled scope and success measures agreed with TAJ before launch.**

## Procurement readiness

Government technology tenders commonly require:

- a valid Tax Compliance Certificate or Letter;
- Public Procurement Commission registration in categories such as Information Technology Services or Computer and Related Services;
- supplier registration on GOJEP;
- technical and financial proposals;
- company registration and banking details;
- references and evidence of capacity;
- security and data-protection documentation.

Before approaching a paid pilot, Lyne should have:

- a registered contracting entity;
- business bank account;
- Tax Compliance Certificate or Letter;
- GOJEP supplier registration;
- relevant PPC registration or a registered delivery partner;
- privacy notice;
- data-processing terms;
- retention and deletion policy;
- incident-response procedure;
- service-level agreement;
- support and continuity plan;
- pilot statement of work;
- professional proposal and price schedule.

## Evidence that the Jamaican market pays for this category

These figures are not direct valuations of Lyne, but they show that Jamaican public entities budget materially for service-delivery and enterprise technology:

- The Government allocated **J$11 billion** to Tax Office construction and modernisation, including expanded online systems.
- TAJ awarded an upgrade of its Revenue Administration Information System for approximately **J$1.866 billion**.
- PICA’s REL-M customer relationship management implementation was publicly described as costing approximately **US$1 million**.
- Public-relations and change-communication services supporting PICA’s CRM implementation were awarded at **J$5.7385 million**.
- The Students’ Loan Bureau issued a national competitive tender specifically for the supply and installation of a queue management system in 2025.

The correct conclusion is not that Lyne should copy the price of a core tax system or CRM. It is that a J$2.75–2.95 million founding pilot is commercially credible and small relative to the expenditure TAJ is already making to improve access and service delivery.
