# QMeNow Granular Feasibility Study

**Prepared:** 31 July 2026  
**Question tested:** Can QMeNow become a financially sustainable Jamaican venture when the present product, remaining launch cost, likely customer prices, founder profile, competition and target institutions are considered together?  
**Planning conversion:** US$1 = J$160.  
**Decision:** **Conditionally feasible.** QMeNow is feasible as a staged, founder-led business that wins a measured private-sector pilot before completing every enterprise feature. It is not feasible as a speculative J$11 million build completed in full before customer proof.

## 1. Executive finding

QMeNow has crossed the line from “idea” to “substantial working product.” Its repository already contains the consumer mobile app, staff and management interface, live queue service, operational database, role and security foundations, forecasting worker and marketing site. This materially reduces technical creation risk.

The venture still has material **commercial proof risk**:

- no live institution has yet proved that QMeNow reduces waiting or improves throughput;
- no paying customer or renewal history exists;
- the founder is early in her professional career and does not yet have a large-company delivery record;
- the leading prospects may already have an internal or contracted queue/appointment product that is not publicly visible;
- a real production deployment, complete device testing and independent security/privacy work remain; and
- the court opportunity overlaps with a national court-modernisation programme and needs scheduled-session capability that is not yet a working end-to-end product.

These risks do not make the venture unworkable. They make **sequence** the central feasibility issue.

> QMeNow should first sell a tightly controlled, one-branch private pilot at approximately J$1.2 million, use measured results to earn a multi-branch contract, and release further capital only when each customer milestone is achieved.

The recommended responsible app-only launch budget is **J$5.66 million (US$35,384)**. The founder-led cash floor is **J$2.44 million (US$15,259)**. The venture can cover its steady operating cost with approximately:

- four one-branch annual customers;
- four properly priced pilots in a year; or
- one well-priced five-branch initial rollout.

Recovering the full J$5.66 million launch investment while also paying the founder a modest salary is likely to take **three to five years in a realistic base case**. A strong multi-branch sale can shorten that period. One discounted one-branch customer cannot carry the business.

## 2. Study scope and method

This study compares five things:

1. the actual state of the local QMeNow repository;
2. the cash required to make that product commercially responsible;
3. current public prices for international queue/customer-flow products;
4. the financial capacity and workflow evidence of the identified Jamaican prospects; and
5. the revenue and customer count required to sustain the venture.

The analysis uses public information, not confidential customer data. Where a prospect’s current queue system cannot be verified publicly, the study records an **unknown**, not an assumption that QMeNow has an open field.

## 3. The product being tested

### 3.1 Present capability

The local repository contains:

- a roughly twenty-screen iPhone/Android customer app;
- remote queue joining, live position, wait estimate and queue updates;
- staff tools to call, verify, serve, skip and manage customers;
- branch manager and executive performance views;
- a walk-in intake role for customers who do not have the app;
- a backend with queue, ticket, staff, branch, prediction, notification, audit and other service areas;
- a MySQL operational database;
- Supabase-based login verification and private file storage;
- a containerised analytics worker that refreshes models approximately every two hours;
- branch/company role restrictions and audit records; and
- a Windows installer configuration and web-installable staff version.

This is enough to support a credible live demonstration of the core flow and enough foundation to justify a limited pilot after the remaining production work.

### 3.2 Material gaps

The repository’s own remaining-work record identifies:

- the clean first-run production setup;
- onboarding guidance;
- some staff-screen filtering, selection and native-use refinements;
- a real-device mobile sweep;
- full end-to-end and persona-based testing;
- accessibility and language assessment;
- production hosting, automatic release, monitoring and backup operations;
- live Apple and Google push-notification credentials;
- formal privacy, retention and deletion documents;
- independent security assurance; and
- public app-store release.

The database contains groundwork for customer web joining, service-readiness information and scheduled sessions. However, the present audit did not find a complete mounted scheduled-session service and usable screens. That means QMeNow can honestly sell **live same-day branch flow** first; it should not yet claim that it fully runs appointment-based court public days.

### 3.3 Product conclusion

Technical feasibility is **high for a one-branch private pilot** and **moderate for a multi-branch commercial release** after the budgeted work. Technical feasibility is **low today for a large public-sector deployment** with strict availability, integration and procurement obligations.

## 4. Market need and customer selection

### 4.1 The need QMeNow must prove

The problem is not merely that a visible line exists. A paying institution must have one or more measurable problems:

- people leave before being served;
- customers arrive without required documents and must return;
- staff cannot see demand early enough to move people between counters;
- wait time differs sharply by branch, day or service;
- online applications still end in an unpredictable in-person handoff;
- managers lack reliable service-time and staffing evidence; or
- the physical waiting area creates crowding, complaint or accessibility risk.

QMeNow is valuable when it changes those outcomes. If a branch has a short, predictable wait or an incumbent system already solves the problem, it is not a good pilot site.

### 4.2 Priority prospect assessment

| Prospect | Evidence of need | Ability to pay | Sales path | Product fit now | Feasibility role |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| First Heritage Credit Union (FHC) | Strategy explicitly links mobile, online and in-branch service; actual queue pain still needs observation | Strong: J$20.49B assets and J$354.34M reported surplus | Named Operations, Member Experience, technology and risk functions are visible | Strong for one branch if no incumbent | **Primary target** |
| Access Financial Services | Branch-based customer/loan-officer workflow; actual queue and incumbent status unknown | Credible private-company buyer | Potential warm route may shorten access | Strong if the current MyAccess app does not already cover arrival/queueing | **Fast-path alternative** |
| Northern Caribbean University Student Finance | Public instructions identify a specific physical registration step for some students | Moderate institutional capacity | Clear operational department, but academic governance can slow approval | Strong for a bounded seasonal/exception workflow | **Second target after private proof** |
| UWI Mona Student Administrative Services | Face-to-face one-stop support is public | Stronger institution, longer approval path | Multiple stakeholders | Good, but governance and integration are heavier | **Later university target** |
| Ultra Medical or similar diagnostic provider | Waiting and appointment coordination are plausible | Private and potentially faster | Smaller buying group | Good for a low-data, high-service pilot after observation | **Parallel discovery option** |
| Traffic Ticket Public Day / Parish Court counter | Public events show very high volume and documented long lines | Government budget exists, but procurement is formal | Court Administration Division, technology, event and procurement stakeholders | Incomplete for scheduled events; potential overlap with IECMS/TIMS | **Strategic discovery, not first revenue** |

No Transport Authority or Traffic Authority opportunity is included. The public-sector analysis is specifically about **Traffic Court and other court administrative services**.

### 4.3 Why FHC ranks first

FHC publicly describes a 2040 direction based on digital transformation, inclusive innovation and sustainable growth. Its stated goal includes seamless experiences across mobile, online and in-branch service and using data to anticipate needs. It also reported J$20.49 billion in assets, J$6.12 billion in loan disbursements and a J$354.34 million net surplus.

Those figures do not prove willingness to buy QMeNow. They do demonstrate that a properly sponsored J$1.2 million pilot is financially possible:

- J$1.2 million is approximately 0.34% of the reported J$354.34 million surplus.
- A J$5.5 million five-branch first-year rollout is approximately 1.55% of that surplus.

The first FHC meeting must answer:

1. Is Qmatic, Wavetec, QLess, Skiplino, Waitwhile, Qminder or an internal equivalent already installed, contracted or being procured?
2. Which branch and service have the most variable and costly wait?
3. Does the existing consultation/callback request create a scheduled appointment, a lead or only a phone call?
4. How often do members arrive without all required information?
5. Can the branch provide four weeks of baseline timestamps or permit QMeNow to measure them?
6. Who has authority to sponsor one branch and sign a limited pilot?

If there is a strong incumbent or no measurable pain, FHC should be disqualified rather than pursued because it is a prestigious name.

### 4.4 Why Access can move ahead

Access can become the first target if the reported warm introduction reaches an operations decision-maker quickly. Early ventures often win through access and trust, not the theoretically highest market score.

The risk is overlap. Access already has a customer-facing MyAccess product and internal digital capability. The discovery call must determine whether arrival, appointment, document readiness, queueing or branch analytics are already present. QMeNow should use the language “customer/client,” “loan officer,” “application status” and “branch service”; it should not use credit-union terms such as “member” unless Access uses them.

### 4.5 Universities

NCU is the strongest first university hypothesis because its Student Finance information identifies an in-person exception process. A seasonal or exception queue is easier to scope than “all university services.”

The buyer is likely to care about:

- reducing crowded registration periods;
- helping students know what to bring;
- separating routine digital cases from exceptions that need a person;
- giving Student Finance a live view of demand; and
- communicating delays or changes without forcing students to stand in one place.

UWI Mona’s one-stop administrative service is strategically attractive but likely requires a broader governance path. UTech is a later option because routine financial clearance is already more automated; the remaining opportunity is exception handling, not general registration.

### 4.6 Courts

Traffic Court has the strongest publicly documented volume pain and the weakest immediate commercial control.

The July 2026 Kingston and St Andrew Traffic Ticket Public Days covered more than 43,000 tickets, required registration and scheduled appointments, and did not accept walk-ins. Jamaica is also implementing the Judicial Integrated Electronic Case Management System (IECMS), intended to cover electronic filing, automated scheduling, case tracking and real-time information.

QMeNow must therefore be positioned narrowly:

> the public arrival, readiness, check-in, live waiting and communication layer before service—not a case-management system and not a replacement for court records.

Before any court build, the Court Administration Division must confirm what IECMS and the Ticket Information Management System already provide. If those programmes include event registration, appointments, arrival check-in, queue display, alerts and operational reporting, QMeNow should not duplicate them.

Government procurement, public accountability, accessibility, security and support requirements also make courts a poor first revenue dependency. The court track is feasible only after:

- a private measured case study;
- the scheduled-session product is working end to end;
- the integration boundary is written down; and
- procurement eligibility and funding source are confirmed.

## 5. Competitive and pricing feasibility

### 5.1 What current international products cost

Public prices show a wide market:

| Product | Public price | Approximate Jamaican value at J$160/US$1 | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Waitwhile Business | starts at US$55/month | starts near J$105,600/year | A low self-service entry point; price rises with visits, locations and enterprise requirements. |
| Skiplino Professional | US$299/month/location | about J$574,080/year/location | A directly relevant queue-management benchmark before local setup, integration and hardware. |
| Skiplino Enterprise | US$399/month/location | about J$766,080/year/location | Adds users, reporting and account support. |
| Qminder Business | US$9,468/year | about J$1,514,880/year | Includes unlimited locations/visitors but limits users and has a mature security/operations position. |
| Qminder Premium | US$12,588/year | about J$2,014,080/year | Adds cross-location views, integration, stronger controls and dedicated support. |

These prices prove that institutions pay recurring amounts for customer-flow systems. They do not prove Jamaican institutions will buy an unproven local product at the same price.

### 5.2 QMeNow’s honest first-customer price

The recommended commercial position is:

| Offer | Recommended price |
|---|---:|
| Opening quote for one-branch founding pilot | **J$1,500,000** |
| Expected negotiated close | **approximately J$1,200,000** |
| Normal floor | **J$900,000** |
| One-branch annual service after proof | **J$900,000–J$1,400,000** |
| Two to five branches, annual platform/support after proof | **J$1,800,000–J$3,200,000** |
| Five-branch initial rollout including configuration/training | **approximately J$4,000,000–J$7,000,000** |

The founding pilot should include:

- one branch;
- up to three services;
- approximately ten to twenty staff users;
- eight to twelve weeks live;
- setup and branch configuration;
- training;
- hosting;
- normal business-hours support; and
- a before-and-after results report.

It should exclude customer hardware, core banking integration, unlimited text messages, custom ownership of QMeNow, major new modules and round-the-clock support.

### 5.3 Why this price is credible despite the founder’s profile

The founder’s limited delivery record and the absence of pilot evidence justify:

- a smaller initial scope;
- milestone payments;
- explicit success measures;
- independent security/privacy review;
- a founding-customer discount; and
- a lower price than an established enterprise vendor.

They do **not** justify a free long-term deployment. A free pilot shifts all risk to the founder, provides weak evidence of willingness to pay and makes later price increases harder.

QMeNow also has genuine positives:

- a substantial functioning code base;
- local language and workflows;
- mobile, web and Windows routes;
- walk-in and remote customers in one operational view;
- per-branch/private deployment;
- forecasting and managerial insight beyond ticket issuance; and
- direct founder access for configuration and support.

The price should therefore reflect an early but real product: below the fully proven enterprise market, above a hobby subscription.

## 7. Unit economics

“Direct delivery cost” means the extra cash spent because a particular customer was won: its hosting, configuration, training, support and customer-specific work. “Contribution” means the contract revenue left after those direct costs; that remaining amount pays general company costs and repays the launch investment.

| Sale type | Base revenue | Estimated direct delivery cost | Contribution | Contribution margin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| One-branch founding pilot | J$1,200,000 | J$350,000 | J$850,000 | 71% |
| One-branch annual service after proof | J$1,100,000 | J$180,000 | J$920,000 | 84% |
| Five-branch initial rollout | J$5,500,000 | J$1,500,000 | J$4,000,000 | 73% |
| Five-branch annual renewal | J$2,800,000 | J$700,000 | J$2,100,000 | 75% |

These margins are attractive only if the scope is controlled. A J$1.2 million pilot becomes unprofitable if QMeNow quietly builds a new banking integration, supplies hardware, offers unlimited on-site support or accepts several bespoke workflows.

### 7.1 Operating break-even

To cover approximately J$3.5 million of annual company cost including modest founder compensation, QMeNow needs roughly one of the following:

- four one-branch annual customers: about J$3.68 million combined contribution;
- four founding pilots: about J$3.4 million combined contribution, just below full break-even;
- one five-branch initial rollout: about J$4 million contribution; or
- one five-branch renewal plus two one-branch annual customers: about J$3.94 million contribution.

This shows why multi-branch credit unions are commercially important. A business built only from occasional one-branch pilots remains fragile.

### 7.2 Launch-investment payback

- The **J$2.44 million founder-led launch** is recovered by approximately three pilots in contribution terms before annual fixed cost, or by two pilots plus one one-branch annual contract. In real cash flow, the founder may need to defer some first-year pay.
- The **J$5.66 million recommended launch** requires roughly seven pilot contributions, or one five-branch initial rollout plus two pilots, before annual company costs.
- The **J$11 million ceiling** would require approximately thirteen pilot contributions. Spending that amount before proof would create an unnecessary financing burden.

The following scenarios are not forecasts. They are decision models showing what must be true for different outcomes.

### 8.2 Base case: feasible but gradual

|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|

It does not fully repay the J$5.66 million recommended launch investment within three years. |---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|

### 8.4 What the scenarios really say

QMeNow does not need dozens of customers to work. It needs:

- the first two pilots to produce evidence;
- at least one multi-branch expansion; and
- disciplined renewal pricing.

The venture is therefore **sales-concentration sensitive**. Losing one five-branch buyer has a large effect. The response is not to build more features in advance; it is to qualify several buyers in parallel while keeping the first deployment narrow.

## 9. Addressable Jamaican opportunity

Public reporting identifies 23 autonomous Jamaican credit unions and more than 100 locations. This is not a massive global market, but it is large enough for an early recurring business:

- ten branches at an average J$1 million annual platform value represent approximately J$10 million recurring revenue;
- twenty branches represent approximately J$20 million; and
- universities, diagnostic centres and selected administrative services add adjacent opportunities.

This is a simplified revenue illustration, not a claim that every location is available. Credit-union consolidation creates both advantages and risks:

- one contract can reach many branches;
- there are fewer major buying decisions;
- post-merger institutions may already be standardising another system; and
- a failed procurement with one large group can remove many branches at once.

The first obtainable market should therefore be defined as:

1. one private branch in financial services;
2. expansion inside that institution;
3. one university/diagnostic reference; and
4. public-sector discovery after proof.

## 10. Founder and delivery feasibility

### 10.1 Founder disadvantage

The founder is graduating, has not delivered a comparable system for a large technology company and has no institutional case study. A buyer may reasonably worry about:

- whether one person can support the system;
- whether security claims have been independently checked;
- what happens if the founder becomes unavailable;
- whether delivery dates and documentation will be professional;
- whether the product will still exist in three years; and
- whether the quoted price was calculated or improvised.

### 10.2 Practical mitigations

These concerns can be reduced without pretending the founder has experience she does not have:

- use a fixed one-branch scope and written exclusions;
- collect payment by milestones, not only at the end;
- bring in a named independent security reviewer;
- keep deployment and recovery instructions in company-controlled documentation;
- create a small advisory group with operations, information security and finance experience;
- have at least one backup technical contractor who can access the production runbook under controlled conditions;
- report pilot results against agreed measures;
- never claim synthetic demo results are customer results; and
- request a reference only after the customer approves the measured report.

The founder’s proximity to the product is also an advantage: fast iteration, direct accountability and lower overhead. Feasibility depends on converting that advantage into repeatable processes before several customers go live.

## 11. Legal, privacy and procurement feasibility

### 11.1 Sole-trader launch

A sole trader can pursue a private pilot. The legal structure is not itself a technical barrier. However:

- the founder is personally responsible for obligations;
- a larger institution may prefer an incorporated contracting party;
- the Apple App Store displays the founder’s personal legal name for an individual/sole-proprietor membership;
- organization app-store accounts require a legal entity and D-U-N-S verification; and
- professional indemnity/cyber insurance may become a customer requirement.

The most feasible route is to complete the sole-trader registration now, but revisit incorporation before a large multi-branch or public-sector agreement.

### 11.2 Data protection

QMeNow handles names, contact information, queue history and potentially identity-related information. A sole trader’s first registration as a data controller currently costs J$7,500 and annual renewal J$5,000.

Feasibility requires:

- collecting only information required for the service;
- clear purposes and retention periods;
- a deletion and correction process;
- written customer/institution responsibilities;
- encrypted transmission and confirmed encrypted storage;
- role-restricted access and audit records;
- a response plan for suspected data exposure; and
- special care before storing identity images.

Identity-document storage should be disabled or tightly limited in a first pilot unless the institution has specifically approved the purpose, data map and retention rule.

### 11.3 Public procurement

The absence of public-procurement documents is not a blocker for a private FHC, Access or clinic discovery/pilot. It is a material blocker for depending on a court contract. Government work may require supplier registration, tax-compliance evidence, procurement classification, tender response capacity and longer payment cycles.

The feasibility conclusion therefore assumes **private first revenue**.

## 12. Operational feasibility and service promise

### 12.1 First pilot support

The first pilot should promise:

- support during agreed branch hours;
- urgent acknowledgement within one business hour;
- a manual branch fallback if QMeNow is unavailable;
- daily backup and tested recovery;
- named persons authorised to request changes; and
- a planned maintenance window.

It should not promise literal zero downtime or round-the-clock engineering support. Those promises are expensive and not credible for a one-person early company.

### 12.2 Large-contract readiness

A large contract requires:

- at least two live application servers;
- a load balancer that can stop sending users to a failed server;
- a database with failover or a documented recovery design;
- separate file storage and backup retention;
- error, availability and security alerts;
- a support rota with more than one trained person;
- a written service-level agreement;
- capacity and failure testing;
- customer-specific information retention; and
- a contract-funded implementation and operating budget.

The estimated annual high-availability operating allowance is approximately **J$4.27–J$6.27 million**, including cloud services and technical support. This must be included in the large customer’s price; it is not part of the pre-customer launch spend.

## 13. Pilot design and proof

### 13.1 Recommended first pilot

- one private branch;
- three services: member/customer onboarding, document/application support and general account/service support;
- online web join plus assisted walk-in entry;
- the mobile app offered but not required;
- no banking transaction data;
- no direct banking-system integration;
- four weeks of baseline measurement;
- eight to twelve weeks live; and
- weekly operational review with the branch sponsor.

### 13.2 Measures

Agree definitions before the pilot:

| Measure | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Median wait | What a typical customer experiences. |
| 90th-percentile wait | The bad-but-not-rare experience; more honest than the average alone. |
| Abandonment | How many people leave before service. |
| Incomplete-document visit | How many people cannot complete the intended service because something was missing. |
| Arrival-to-service time by channel | Whether remote and walk-in customers are handled fairly. |
| Staff utilisation by service window | Whether capacity is positioned where demand actually exists. |
| Customer confidence score | Whether people understand their status and what happens next. |

Do not promise a specific percentage improvement before baseline data exists. The pilot agreement can set a decision threshold such as:

- a statistically and operationally meaningful wait reduction;
- no worsening of walk-in fairness;
- a decrease in incomplete visits; and
- acceptable staff adoption.

### 13.3 Payment schedule

A credible J$1.2 million pilot can use:

- 40% on signature and scheduling: J$480,000;
- 30% when the branch configuration and staff training are accepted: J$360,000;
- 20% after four weeks live: J$240,000; and
- 10% on the agreed results report: J$120,000.

This protects both sides and prevents the founder financing the institution’s pilot alone.

## 14. Staged investment decision

### Stage 1 — qualification and account readiness

**Maximum new spend: J$350,000.**

Required outputs:

- sole-trader and data-controller registration confirmed;
- DigitalOcean, Supabase, Expo, Apple/Google route selected;
- two independent specialist quotes;
- at least ten discovery conversations;
- two qualified operational sponsors; and
- one branch willing to supply or permit baseline measurement.

### Stage 2 — pilot-ready production

**Maximum additional spend: J$1.2 million.**

Release only after:

- a signed paid pilot, letter of intent with budget or grant;
- one defined branch and three defined services;
- written confirmation of no blocking incumbent;
- agreed data and privacy boundary; and
- agreed success measures.

Complete:

- customer web join/readiness flow;
- production hosting and recovery;
- real-device corrections;
- staff configuration;
- core security corrections; and
- pilot training.

### Stage 3 — full assurance and public release

Release the balance of the J$5.66 million plan only when the pilot or grant justifies:

- independent security assessment;
- full privacy/contract package;
- accessibility assessment;
- public app-store release;
- Windows signing;
- stronger automated testing; and
- first post-launch support reserve.

### Stage 4 — expansion

Do not build court scheduled sessions, custom core integrations or high-availability public infrastructure until the relevant customer has:

- confirmed the missing capability;
- specified the integration boundary;
- provided a realistic budget; and
- signed a contract or paid discovery engagement.

## 15. Go, pause and stop rules

### Continue

Continue funding when, within 90 days:

- ten relevant discovery meetings have occurred;
- at least two prospects confirm measurable branch pain;
- at least one prospect confirms there is no blocking incumbent;
- a branch sponsor permits observation or baseline data; and
- the pilot price is discussed with a person who controls or influences budget.

### Pause and reposition

Pause feature spending if:

- buyers like the demo but will not provide a branch or baseline;
- the only interest is government without procurement readiness;
- prospects already have queue systems but need a narrow integration; or
- the customer values readiness/check-in more than live queueing.

In that case, sell a paid operational study or narrow the product rather than continuing a general build.

### Stop or pivot

Stop the present commercial approach if, after approximately 180 days of disciplined outreach:

- no prospect is willing to sign a pilot at J$900,000 or more;
- all credible targets already have satisfactory incumbents;
- buyers require integrations and certifications far beyond the attainable price;
- branch observations show no costly queue/readiness problem; or
- support expectations make each deal structurally unprofitable.

Stopping a market approach is not the same as deleting the product. The underlying platform may be repositioned for a narrower workflow with proven demand.

## 17. Final feasibility verdict

### Is QMeNow feasible?

**Yes—conditionally.**

It is feasible because:

- the core product already exists;
- the remaining first-launch hosting bill is small relative to Jamaican institutional software prices;
- current international products establish a real paid category;
- a J$1.2 million pilot and J$900,000–J$1.4 million annual branch price are commercially credible;
- FHC and other large institutions have the financial capacity to buy; and
- one multi-branch rollout can cover the business’s annual operating requirement.

It is conditional because:

- queue pain and incumbent status are not yet verified at the named branches;
- the founder has no live institutional case study;
- the production, assurance and release gaps are material;
- a single one-branch customer is not enough to sustain the company; and
- public-sector work has procurement and system-overlap risk.

### The financially correct decision

1. Do **not** spend J$11 million before proof.
2. Treat **J$2.44 million** as the founder-led external-cash floor.
3. Raise or reserve **J$5.66 million** as the responsible full app budget, but release it in stages.
4. Target **FHC and the warm Access route first**, with NCU as the first university follow-on.
5. Quote **J$1.5 million**, seek to close near **J$1.2 million**, and avoid going below **J$900,000** without exceptional value in return.
6. Require baseline access, a named branch sponsor and a clean incumbent check before customer-specific work.
7. Keep Traffic Court in discovery until scheduled sessions work and the IECMS/TIMS boundary is confirmed.
8. Measure the first pilot and sell the next contract on evidence, not projections.

Under that sequence, QMeNow is a reasonable venture to continue. Without that sequence, it is an expensive unproven build.
